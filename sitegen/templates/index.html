The same data show very different patterns across states: who dies (pedestrians, motorcyclists, unrestrained occupants), where (rural roads, roadway departures), and how long it takes to reach care. Practitioners need a quick, transparent way to see their state's pattern and to find the published evidence that addresses it.

The Safe System approach, in brief

The U.S. DOT National Roadway Safety Strategy (2022) adopts the Safe System Approach, which "acknowledges both human mistakes and human vulnerability, and designs a redundant system to protect everyone" (NRSS p. 6). Its five objectives (NRSS p. 11, paraphrased):

Safe Roads

Roadway design that accounts for mistakes and injury tolerances.

Safe Speeds

Safer speeds through context-appropriate design, outreach, and enforcement.

Safe Vehicles

Vehicle systems that prevent crashes and protect people inside and outside.

Safe Road Users

Safe, responsible behavior by everyone using the road.

Post-Crash Care

Fast access to emergency medical care and safe incident management.

This tool lists upstream, system-level resources (roads, speeds, vehicles) first, then road-user strategies, then post-crash care.

How it works

1 · Sources

Public data

NHTSA FARS final bulk files, Census population estimates, FHWA VM-2 VMT. URLs + checksums in sources.yml.

2 · Ingest

Verify & stage

Python discovers files from NHTSA's listing, checks size/MD5/SHA-256, stages columns to Parquet.

3 · Transform

DuckDB + dbt

State × year measures, rates, peers, EMS times; tests reconcile states with national totals and NHTSA benchmarks.

4 · Rules

Signals → resources

Fixed YAML rules compare each state with the median of states and link tagged, cited resources.

5 · Outputs

App & site

Streamlit app and this static site, both read the committed aggregates (no raw data, no AI).
